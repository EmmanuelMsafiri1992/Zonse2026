title: Notes, OKRs, issues, meeting minutes, client portal and freelancer get real rules
---

- **Notes & whiteboards**: a whiteboard note needs its image, and a checklist needs items. Checklists written with `[ ]` and `[x]` lines count how many items are done. An archived note can't be edited until it's reopened. The home screen shows open checklist items and the notes shared with the team, and reports show checklist progress.
- **Goals & OKR tracking**: key results measure progress from their start value to their target, including targets that go down, such as a churn rate, and a key result is marked achieved once it hits the target. Objectives average their key results. An objective can't be marked achieved while key results are still open, and a team objective must name its team. Each night, objectives due within two weeks that are under 70% are flagged at risk, and overdue ones are flagged off track.
- **Issue tracking & roadmap**: high and critical bugs need steps to reproduce. A release can't ship while its issues are still open, and a shipped release takes no new issues. Releases show how much of their scope is closed. Shipping one writes its release notes from the finished issues. Open issues can be triaged in one click. Reports show open issues by priority, progress per release and time to close.
- **Meeting minutes & action items**: a meeting can't be marked held before it happens, and it needs its attendees. Approving the minutes locks them. Action items can't be due before the meeting, and they can be completed in one click. Meetings count their open actions, and everyone sees their own open actions on the home screen.
- **Client portal**: a deliverable sent for approval needs a file or preview link, and asking for changes needs the client's feedback. A project can't be completed until every deliverable is approved, and a completed project takes no new deliverables or updates. Projects show their sign-off progress, deliverables and latest updates.
- **Freelancer workspace**: work can't start until the contract is signed, a proposal needs its text, and a paid gig needs an invoice number that isn't used elsewhere. Time can only be logged on gigs that are under way, and no more than 24 hours at a time. Hourly and daily gigs are valued from the time logged, at 8 hours to a day. "Mark paid" records the invoice and marks the gig's time billed. Reports show income by client and hours by gig.
