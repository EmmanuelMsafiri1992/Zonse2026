title: Police dockets, land titles, civil registration, tenders and traffic fines get real rules
---

- **Police occurrence book & case dockets**:
  - Every occurrence-book entry has one OB number and is never dated in the future.
  - A case docket opened from an entry shows on that entry. The docket then moves from investigation to the prosecutor and on to court.
  - Exhibits are booked against a docket, and a docket is finalised only once every exhibit has been returned or disposed of.
  - Home shows the oldest open dockets. Reports show occurrences by month, dockets by status and exhibits held by store.
- **Land registry & title deeds**:
  - Every title deed has one title number.
  - Only the current owner can lodge a transfer, and only one transfer per title may be in progress. An encumbered title cannot be transferred until its bond is cancelled.
  - When the transfer is registered, the title moves to the buyer and the transfer history is recorded.
  - Reports show titles by location, and transfers by month and by conveyancer.
- **Civil registry (births, deaths, marriages)**:
  - Each registration gets a number made from its type and year.
  - The event cannot be dated after its registration, and a registration made more than a year after the event is flagged as late.
  - Certificates are issued and counted, an amendment needs a reason, and a cancelled registration cannot issue certificates.
  - Reports show registrations by type and by place, and vital statistics by month.
- **E-procurement & tenders**:
  - A tender is published with a unique number and a closing date. It takes bids only while it is open and closes by itself after that date.
  - Bids are checked for compliance (tax compliance is required) and scored out of 100 from their technical, price and preference points.
  - The tender is awarded to the highest-scoring compliant bid, and the other bids are marked unsuccessful.
  - Home shows tenders closing soon. Reports show the bid ranking, awards and tenders by category.
- **Traffic fines & vehicle licensing**:
  - Registrations are unique and kept in capitals. A vehicle's licence expires by itself after its expiry date.
  - A renewal extends the licence by a year from today or from the old expiry, whichever is later. A vehicle with unpaid warrants cannot be renewed.
  - Fines are due by their pay-by date and become warrants once overdue. A contested fine is either upheld or cancelled.
  - Reports show fines by offence, location and month.
