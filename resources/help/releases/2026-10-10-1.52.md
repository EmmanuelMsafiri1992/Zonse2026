title: Cleaning, consultancy, car wash, tailoring, printing and pet grooming apps get real rules
---

- **Cleaning & home services**: jobs can only be booked at active addresses, and a cleaner can't start two jobs at the same time on the same day. A job can't be marked done without its hours. Finishing a job at a weekly, fortnightly or monthly address books the next visit. Scheduled jobs whose day has passed are marked as missed overnight. The report shows jobs, hours and missed visits for each cleaner.
- **Consultancy & client portals**: deliverables can only be added to proposals and active engagements. A deliverable is sent to the client with a file link. Rejecting one needs the client's feedback, and it then goes back for rework. An engagement can't be completed until every deliverable is approved.
- **Car wash**: a wash charges its package's price unless another amount is given. It also earns the washer the package's commission, which can't be more than the price. Only active packages can be sold, and registrations are stored in capitals. The report shows each washer's commission.
- **Tailoring & alterations**: a new garment needs a current measurement card, and adding a new card for a customer marks their older cards as outdated. The deposit can't be more than the price, and the fitting must be on or before the ready-by date. An order moves from cutting and sewing to fitting, ready and collected, and the balance is shown at collection.
- **Print shop**: a job needs a quantity of at least one. A proof can only be sent with the artwork link. Nothing goes to print until the customer approves the proof and has paid at least half the price as a deposit. A rejected proof goes back to artwork.
- **Pet grooming & boarding**: appointments can only be booked for active pets. Boarding and daycare need vaccinations that are valid for the whole stay, and a boarding stay needs a collect-by date after the drop-off date. A visit is checked in, worked on, marked ready and then collected, and each pet shows its visit history.
